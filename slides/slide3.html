2. Few labels? Rank with a weak learner

Nair et al. (arXiv 1702.05701): label a few rows, let kNN rank the rest, check the top 5, report where the best of them really ranks

x is free, y is dear. One label = one build, one benchmark, one expert hour — so count labels, not CPU. And never ask the model for a number: ask it for a shortlist. Optimization needs the tail, not the function, so a guesser too weak to predict is still strong enough to rank.

pipeline: split Seed=s N=budget → ydist → knnr → rank C=5

        Cost = N + 5 labels: N to train the guess, 5 to check its shortlist. Nothing else is ever labelled.

    

  


  

    

      
rank.awk (post: true rank of the best of the top C)

      

    

    

      

        rank of best checked row, % (lower = better; 1 = top 1%): 10 MOOT files × 50 shuffles, 50:50 train:test
        

          dataerr %ρ
          rand2550100
        

        
      

      

        err %, ρ: how much the knn@25 guess knows — 100·MAE / MAE(predict the mean of the 25 labels),
        and Spearman ρ of guessed vs actual over every test row. A good predictor would score 0 and 1.00.

        rand = no learner: label 5 rows at random, keep the best. 25 / 50 / 100 = labels spent before checking 5.

        bold = lowest, or within 0.35·sd of it (sd over every run for that file).
      

      
Read the gap between err % and the budget columns: the guess is bad at values and
        mediocre at order, yet its top-5 lands in the best few percent — and 25 labels buys nearly all of it.
