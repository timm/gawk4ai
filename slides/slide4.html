4. Rare classes? Complement NB on text

About 1% of these papers are a “yes”, so accuracy is useless — answer “no” to everything and score 99%. Count recall instead. Ten labels get most of the way; spending more can make it worse.

min.py — tf-idf picks the 64 words that carry most


    
No stemmer, a 16-word stop list, and the abstract only
    — the same choices the gawk version makes.

  

  

    
min.py — complement naive bayes

    

    
Score a class by what it is not like.  With 45 yes
    against 1659 no, the complement of “yes” is estimated from
    plenty of data; the class itself is not.

Columns are labels spent.  The warm start is ten rows,
      half of them forced to be yes; after that the learner asks for whatever
      looks most like a yes.

      
Which is the problem.  Labelling the likeliest yes first
      starves the “no” class, and complement NB needs that side to
      be well estimated — so on Kitchenham and Radjenovic the curve falls
      as the budget grows.  A fixed held-out half rules out the easy
      explanation: this is the method, not the measurement.
