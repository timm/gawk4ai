5. How many papers must someone read?

Same pipeline as the last slide, with two knobs: some.awk keeps only the first N labelled papers, abcd.awk Every=10 prints a running macro pd as the guesses arrive. So one run reports both axes — how much reading buys the score, and how much of the rest you must check to see it. pd, not accuracy: ~1% of these papers are a “yes”, so accuracy rewards saying “no” to everything. Mean of 10 shuffles.

Here, labels really do pay. At 50 papers every data set sits at pd 50–54 — that is chance, the model answering “no” to everything while accuracy reads a flattering 88%. 100 papers buys 55–57, 200 buys 57–67, and only the full training half — 850 papers for Kitchenham, 4450 for Hall — reaches 76.8–85.6. Nothing plateaus on the way up: every doubling is still paying when the budget runs out. Rare classes are the case where you cannot shortcut the reading.

The test side is still cheap. Hall is steady after 470 of its 4450 held-out papers, Wahono after 200 of 3500, Radjenovic after 160 of 3000. Scoring is not what costs; labelling is.
